Check your downloaded app

        
      

      

        

          Check that your saved app matches a published release before opening
          it.
        

        
1. Get the checksum

        

          Download paperwarden.html and
          paperwarden.html.sha256 from the
          matching GitHub release
              (opens in a new tab). If you already have the app, you only need its matching checksum
          file.
        

        
Keep both files in the same folder.

        
2. Verify the file

        
Open Terminal or PowerShell in that folder and run:

        

          
macOS

          
shasum -a 256 -c paperwarden.html.sha256

        

        

          
Windows (PowerShell)

          
try {
  $expected = (Get-Content .\paperwarden.html.sha256 -Raw -ErrorAction Stop).Split()[0]
  $actual = (Get-FileHash .\paperwarden.html -Algorithm SHA256 -ErrorAction Stop).Hash
  if ($actual -eq $expected) { "OK" } else { "FAILED" }
} catch {
  "FAILED"
}

        

        

          
Linux

          
sha256sum -c paperwarden.html.sha256

        

        
3. Check the result

        

          OK means the file matches the checksum. If the check
          fails, don't use the app.
        

        

          A matching checksum confirms the file matches the published release,
          not that the software is safe. Download the checksum from the official
          GitHub release.
        

      

    
    
      

        
About Paperwarden

        
      

      

        

          Keep what you can’t afford to lose on paper: master passwords,
          recovery codes, private keys, instructions for your family. A
          saved copy of the app
          opens it later, with no website or internet required.
        

        

          Free and
          open source (opens in a new tab), so anyone can check how it works.
        

        
Why paper?

        

          Backup drives fail, phones get stolen and accounts get locked. Paper
          needs no power, no subscription, and no company staying in business.
          Printed on good paper and stored away from light and moisture, it can
          last for decades.
        

        

          Fire, water, and loss are still risks, so keep spare copies in
          different places. It’s a good way to leave your family what they’d
          need if something happened to you.
        

        
Make and recover a backup

        

          Enter one or two messages, each with its own strong password. Both
          messages share one QR code. Choose Save PDF, then print
          without shrinking or enlarging the page. Keep the white margin around
          the QR code intact.
        

        

          Keep the passwords separate from the printed paper. Before storing the
          paper, test that you can recover your message from a photo or scan of
          the actual print. If you created two messages, test both.
        

        

          To recover, open your saved app, import the QR image or text copy, and
          enter the password. The four-word Backup ID helps you tell your
          backups apart.
        

        

          Scanning the QR with your phone's camera opens the website. This is a
          convenience for casual use; for important backups, use your saved app
          offline.
        

        

          Use the non-sensitive notes area on the printout to handwrite what the
          paper is and where to find the saved app. Don't write passwords or
          other secrets there. Your family may never have heard of Paperwarden,
          and scanning the QR with a phone only works while the website exists.
        

        

          If the app is lost, the printout includes technical instructions for
          rebuilding a compatible reader.
        

        
Using it safely

        

          Anyone who gets their hands on the paper can keep guessing the
          password, as many times as they like, on their own computer. Use the
          dice button to generate a random eight-word password, or choose your
          own strong one. If you use two passwords, don’t base one on the other.
        

        

          Putting two messages in one backup, each with its own password, gives
          you plausible deniability: someone who knows only one can’t tell
          whether the other exists. It can’t make anyone believe you, so don’t
          rely on it if your safety is at risk.
        

        

          Use a trusted device, verify your downloaded app, and go offline
          before entering secrets. Paperwarden is experimental and has not had
          an independent security audit.
